# Product and Operations Core Workflow

## Module metadata

- **Module ID:** OPS-001
- **Category:** Product and operations
- **Version:** 1.0.0-draft
- **Status:** Initial core Reference
- **Triggers:** Product design, MVP, supplier, production, fulfillment, operations, experiment design
- **Exclusions:** Strategy discussion without product or operating implications
- **Dependencies:** Portable control rules; customer problem, constraints, and available resources

## Purpose

Translate a customer need and strategy into a product or service that can be tested and delivered within the venture's real constraints.

## Workflow

1. **Define the user and use case.** Specify who uses or buys the offer, the situation, and the job to be done.
2. **Set scope.** Separate must-have outcomes from optional features. Describe the smallest testable offer and what it intentionally excludes.
3. **Map delivery.** Outline sourcing, production or service steps, quality checks, packaging, fulfillment, support, and capacity as relevant.
4. **Identify constraints and risks.** Check skills, time, cash, supplier dependence, reliability, safety, privacy, accessibility, and jurisdiction-specific requirements where applicable. Flag unresolved compliance questions rather than guessing.
5. **Design a validation experiment.** State the hypothesis, target participants, method, measure, threshold or decision rule, duration, cost, and what different outcomes imply.
6. **Plan iteration.** Record results and operational learning; update product scope and affected financial or strategic assumptions.

## Output

Provide product/service scope, user journey or process, resource and supplier assumptions, risks, experiment plan, measures, and dependencies. Distinguish a prototype, pilot, and production-ready offer.

## Quality and risk checks

- The proposed solution addresses a specific customer need and is testable.
- Capacity, cost, lead time, and quality assumptions are explicit.
- Safety and regulatory needs are identified early for the applicable activity and location.
- Experiments measure behavior or credible commitment where possible, not only stated enthusiasm.
- Avoid presenting a proposed supplier, process, or compliance status as verified unless evidence exists.

## Integration

Use customer evidence from Market Research and strategic priorities from Business Strategy. Provide quantified cost/capacity inputs to Financial Analysis. Feed experiments and outcomes into the relevant project gate and evidence registry.
