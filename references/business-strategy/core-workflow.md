# Business Strategy Core Workflow

## Module metadata

- **Module ID:** STR-001
- **Category:** Business strategy
- **Version:** 1.0.0-draft
- **Status:** Initial core Reference
- **Triggers:** Positioning, value proposition, business model, go-to-market, SWOT, marketing mix
- **Exclusions:** Standalone arithmetic checks with no strategic question
- **Dependencies:** Portable control rules; relevant project decisions and market evidence

## Purpose

Turn customer and market understanding into a coherent set of choices about whom to serve, what value to offer, how to deliver it, and how the venture can capture value.

## Workflow

1. **Establish the starting point.** Read the project profile, confirmed decisions, target customers, and relevant market evidence. Mark missing or stale inputs.
2. **Define the customer and problem.** Specify the segment, use situation, job or need, current alternative, and cost of the unresolved problem.
3. **Form the value proposition.** State the customer, need, offer, differentiated benefit, and reason to believe. Separate proven differentiation from a hypothesis.
4. **Map the business model.** Identify value creation, delivery, revenue source, pricing basis, key costs, channels, partners, resources, and major risks. Test that these elements fit together.
5. **Compare strategic options.** For each viable option, state fit with customer evidence, capability requirements, economics, risks, reversibility, and key assumptions. Do not silently choose when the decision belongs to the founder.
6. **Translate choices into action.** Define positioning, a bounded go-to-market test, success measures, and the decisions that must be made next.

## Output

Deliver the strategic choice or options, supporting evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, rejected alternatives where useful, decision owner, and next validation action. For SWOT or 4P requests, connect each item to an implication rather than listing labels without analysis.

## Quality and risk checks

- The target segment is specific enough to guide product and channel choices.
- The claimed advantage matters to that segment and has a plausible basis.
- Revenue logic and cost structure do not contradict the proposed positioning.
- Confirmed decisions remain intact unless the user requested a change; analyze downstream effects.
- Do not describe aspiration as an established competitive advantage.

## Integration

Use Market Research evidence as inputs; send product and channel implications to Product & Operations; pass price, volume, and cost assumptions to Financial Analysis; record user-approved strategic choices in the decision log.
