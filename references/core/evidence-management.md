# Evidence Management

## Evidence record

Store a stable evidence ID, the exact claim it bears on, source type, source reference, publisher when known, publication date when known, observation/access date from the runtime, relevant scope, confidence assessment, and limitations.

## Source handling

- Prefer original documents, official statistics, research papers, and direct customer observations for claims they can support.
- Preserve the source and its scope; do not generalize a local or dated finding to a different population without justification.
- Distinguish primary, secondary, user-provided, and internally calculated material.
- Record contradictions instead of deleting them. A source may support one claim and contradict another.
- Do not fabricate publication dates, access dates, quotes, or source contents.

## Confidence

Use confidence labels only as a concise assessment of relevance, method, recency, and corroboration; explain major limitations. Confidence is not a probability unless a defined statistical method supports that interpretation.

## Claims in reports

Link material findings to evidence IDs or sources. Label inference and estimate. Evidence from desk research alone does not establish willingness to pay, actual demand, or operational feasibility.
